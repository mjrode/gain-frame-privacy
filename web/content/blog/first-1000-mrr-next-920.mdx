RevenueCat shows **$1,920.18 MRR** and **330 active subscriptions** for GainFrame this morning. I crossed $1,000.04 on July 12. The app launched on March 8, so the first $1,000 took 126 days. The next $920.14 took 31.

That is the part I keep staring at. The second leg has moved much faster than the first, and it happened during the month when my free web tools stopped looking like side projects and started looking like an acquisition channel.

The revenue numbers are public on [RevenueCat's verified page](https://verified.revenuecat.com/gainframe). Search data comes from the `sc-domain:gainframe.app` property in Google Search Console, with final data through August 9. Today's RevenueCat value is still a live snapshot. MRR can move in either direction as subscriptions start and churn.

## The second leg is running at 3.7 times the daily pace

The simple arithmetic is $1,000 divided by 126 days, then $920.14 divided by 31 days.

| Window | MRR added | Days | MRR added per day |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Launch to July 12 | $1,000.04 | 126 | $7.94 |
| July 12 to August 12 | $920.14 | 31 | $29.68 |

That makes the recent pace about **3.7 times faster** per day. It does not mean the business grew in a clean straight line. RevenueCat normalizes monthly and annual subscriptions into MRR, renewals and churn land on uneven days, and the recent curve includes a sharp jump from August 8 to August 11.

I also have a timing problem if I try to tell a clean story about cause. Product changes, Reddit posts, TikTok traffic, organic search, trial conversion, and older cohorts all overlap. I can verify when the line moved. I cannot assign each new dollar to one channel.

I can show what happened to search during the same period.

## Free tools became one third of organic search

I compared the 28 days immediately before the $1,000 milestone with the next 28 days. That keeps the search windows equal and avoids comparing one good week with a full month.

| Google Search Console | Jun 15 to Jul 12 | Jul 13 to Aug 9 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Site clicks | 2,557 | 7,902 | +209% |
| Site impressions | 105,530 | 422,057 | +300% |
| Free-tool clicks | 298 | 2,499 | **+739%** |
| Free-tool impressions | 3,866 | 22,552 | **+483%** |
| Tools' share of site clicks | 11.7% | 31.6% | +19.9 points |

The site grew fast. The [free tools directory](/tools/) grew much faster. Nearly one in three organic clicks now lands somewhere under `/tools/`, up from roughly one in nine in the prior window.

<figure className="post-render-figure scroll-reveal">
  <img src="assets/tool-clicks-by-week.webp" alt="Weekly Google Search Console clicks to GainFrame free tools rising from 19 in mid June to 1,013 in early August" loading="lazy" />
  <p className="post-caption">Weekly tool clicks went from 19 in mid June to 1,013 in the week ending August 9.</p>
</figure>

The branded share moved the right way too. Known searches for GainFrame produced 694 clicks in the recent window, about 8.8% of site clicks. Residual non-brand clicks rose from 2,176 to 7,208, a **231% increase**. Query privacy filtering means that split is conservative, but this was not a brand-search spike wearing an SEO costume.

GA4 tells the same directional story with a different unit. Organic sessions rose from 2,928 to 8,765, engaged sessions rose from 1,748 to 5,715, and engagement rate improved from 59.7% to 65.2%. GSC clicks and GA4 sessions are separate metrics, so I keep them separate. The agreement is useful; the counts should not be combined.

## The existing tools did more work than the new ones

I added three tools after the $1,000 milestone:

- [AI Body Transformation](/tools/ai-body-transformation/) on July 18
- [AI Physique Rater](/tools/physique-rater/) on July 23
- [Body Visualizer](/tools/body-visualizer/) on August 6

The directory went from 11 live tools to 14. It would be easy to credit the traffic growth to publishing three new pages. The page report says roughly **79% of the added tool clicks came from pages that already existed** on July 12.

<figure className="post-render-figure scroll-reveal">
  <img src="assets/tool-page-winners.webp" alt="Matched 28-day comparison of organic clicks to GainFrame's body fat photo tool, body fat visualizer, physique rater, and tools directory" loading="lazy" />
  <p className="post-caption">Four pages produced 98% of all tool clicks. Two of the three biggest winners already existed at $1,000 MRR.</p>
</figure>

The [body-fat-from-photo tool](/tools/body-fat-from-photo/) went from **203 to 1,376 clicks**. Its CTR barely changed, 10.63% to 10.79%, while average position improved from 7.58 to 5.87 and impressions rose from 1,910 to 12,756. The growth came from showing up more often and ranking better, not a headline trick.

The [body-fat visualizer](/tools/body-fat-visualizer/) went from **12 to 446 clicks**. CTR improved from 9.16% to 13.21%, while average position moved from 10.02 to 7.53. Its head query, "body fat visualizer," produced 133 clicks at position 4.06 in the recent window.

The Physique Rater is the breakout new page. It produced **419 clicks from 1,767 impressions**, a 23.7% CTR at average position 4.04. On July 23 I expanded the number of pages linking to it from about 3 to 23. On July 31 I retargeted the copy around "rate my physique," "rate my body," and "body rater" after Search Console showed the original query split. The traffic breakout began around July 31. Those dates line up, but the data does not prove the links or retargeting caused it.

The other two new tools need restraint. AI Body Transformation produced 32 page clicks in the window. Body Visualizer produced 17 clicks and 647 impressions after launching on August 6. Google indexed it in under a day, but its 28-day measurement window does not close until September 3.

The classic calculators produced almost nothing. TDEE, macros, FFMI, calorie deficit, calories burned, one-rep max, and strength standards combined for only a handful of clicks. Four pages generated 98% of tool traffic. The total tool count is a weak metric by itself.

## The Git history shows a month of compounding fixes

I pulled the repository history from July 12 through August 12 and matched the meaningful changes against the traffic timeline.

<figure className="post-render-figure scroll-reveal">
  <img src="assets/repo-changes-timeline.webp" alt="Timeline of GainFrame tool releases, funnel improvements, internal linking work, and content inventory changes from July 12 to August 12" loading="lazy" />
  <p className="post-caption">Three new tools mattered. The month also included funnel work, search retargeting, hub cleanup, and 424 additional internal links.</p>
</figure>

The sequence matters because most of the work was not a new URL.

**July 13: I rebuilt the photo estimator result screen.** It had four competing dark blocks and buried the App Store download behind them. I reduced it to one decision area with the download and email report together. That was funnel work after the search click, not SEO copy.

**July 18: I shipped AI Body Transformation.** The launch commit also linked the new tool from six relevant blog posts and two sibling tool surfaces. It received a real place in the graph on day one.

**July 23: I shipped and interlinked the Physique Rater.** A second commit featured it on the tools hub, added it to related-tool footers across the calculator set, and linked it from seven relevant articles. Exported pages linking to it moved from about 3 to 23.

**July 29: I reordered the tools hub around real entry traffic.** The ItemList schema had a duplicate position that invalidated every item after it. I fixed the numbering, moved the visualizer ahead of low-traffic calculators, changed the title to match the pages that already ranked, and rewrote cross-links based on entry-session behavior.

**July 31 through August 1: I retargeted queries and repaired the content graph.** The first full audit found 46 orphan pages, 929 internal links, and a median of three inbound links. One pass took the graph to 1,279 links and eight orphans. The current inventory is 1,353 links, seven orphans, and a median of four. All seven remaining orphans are founder or product-news posts that are distributed directly rather than judged on search equity.

**August 6: I shipped Body Visualizer after the audit found a new query family.** "Body visualizer" showed 40,500 monthly searches, 22% yearly growth, and low reported keyword difficulty. The related family was roughly 86,000 monthly searches. I kept it separate from the body-fat visualizer so I did not disturb a page already ranking for a more specific query.

This is the part I missed when I first started publishing tools. A useful page is only one piece. It needs a query with the right intent, links from pages Google already understands, a clear next step after the result, and enough untouched time to measure what happened.

## The audit loop made me ship less blindly

I [wrote the mechanics of the weekly SEO audit loop](/blog/seo-weekly-audit-loop/) a few days ago. The short version is that every run pulls Search Console, checks market data, inspects every local page and link, proposes a small set of changes, waits for my approval, then records what shipped and when it can be judged.

The effect on the tool strategy has been specific:

- Stop adding generic calculators because the existing ones have almost no search demand on this site.
- Improve the pages already earning clicks before widening the directory.
- Add a tool only when the query intent calls for interaction, as it did for Physique Rater and Body Visualizer.
- Treat internal linking as launch work, not cleanup for later.
- Leave a new page alone for 28 days unless something is broken.

The process also killed work. I froze an 11-post measurement-stats cluster after market data showed several target keywords declining 33% to 66% a year with 0.3% to 1.0% CTR. I reverted a body-fat chart title test after it lost cleanly. I skipped adjacent workout and nutrition ideas when the search results showed the wrong shopper.

The best output from the loop is often a smaller shipping list.

## The revenue line and the search line still do not join

The dates are compelling. From July 12 to August 12, MRR added $920.14. Across the matched Search Console windows around that milestone, free-tool clicks added 2,201 and the tools' share of site clicks rose from 11.7% to 31.6%.

I still cannot say those 2,201 extra clicks produced a specific number of subscriptions.

The correct PostHog website project has no recent events, which means successful tool results and outbound App Store clicks are unavailable for this window. GA4 gives me sessions and engagement. Search Console gives me queries, pages, and clicks. RevenueCat gives me subscriptions and MRR. I do not have a reliable user-level bridge across those systems.

TikTok is still active. Reddit posts send bursts of downloads. Trial conversion work from before the $1,000 milestone is still affecting later cohorts. The app itself kept shipping. Annual subscriptions can create MRR jumps that do not resemble the website traffic curve.

My working inference is that the free tools became a meaningful part of acquisition during a month when MRR accelerated. The evidence supports the timing, scale, and page-level search growth. It does not support a clean revenue attribution claim.

That instrumentation gap is the next fix. I want successful tool result, App Store click, install, trial, and paid events connected well enough to answer which pages create customers rather than which pages create visits.

<hr className="post-divider" />

## I am still $79.82 short of the milestone

I have not hit $2,000 MRR yet. At $1,920.18, a good day can cross it and a bad churn day can move it away again. I would rather publish the current number than round up early.

The useful part is already visible. The first $1,000 took 126 days. The next $920.14 took 31. During those 31 days, the tools directory grew from 11 pages to 14, organic tool clicks rose 739% across matched windows, and pages that already existed produced most of the incremental traffic.

I plan to keep the next month boring: repair conversion instrumentation, protect the three search winners, wait for the open measurement windows, and improve tools people already use before adding more.

> The first $1,000 took 126 days. The next $920.14 took 31. I know exactly when both lines moved. I still need better instrumentation before I claim why.
