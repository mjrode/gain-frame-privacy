You found a 12-week program you like, but the spreadsheet has one tab for percentages, another for substitutions and a separate notes file for what you actually lifted. This Boostcamp review asks whether putting the program and the log together solves that problem without making the plan harder to understand.

Boostcamp offers named coach programs, community programs and a workout tracker, with extra planning and analysis in Pro. It also supports social workout photos. The distinction worth keeping is between a record of training work, a shared workout image and a standardized visual check-in.

This is a sourced evaluation checked October 27, 2026. We reviewed official pages and the current US listing; we did not complete a training cycle in the app. The images are publisher screenshots, and the review sheet below is an original template for your own use.

## Who is Boostcamp best for in this review?

Boostcamp is a strong candidate if you want to follow a structured lifting program in the same place you log it. The [official program library](https://www.boostcamp.app/programs) lets you explore plans by goals and schedule. Familiar names are a starting point, but the weekly commitment, exercise selection and progression rules still need to fit your circumstances.

A named program can make the next session easier to interpret: today's work belongs to a longer plan. That is especially useful if you tend to improvise every session and then struggle to explain whether the block worked. It is less useful if browsing keeps persuading you to abandon one reasonable program for another.

**Platform:** [iOS App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/boostcamp-workout-tracker/id1529354455) and [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bpmhealth.boostcamp). **US rating checked October 27:** 4.85/5 from 10,702 US ratings.

## What is free, and what does Boostcamp Pro add?

The [current Pro page](https://www.boostcamp.app/pro) separates a free program library and workout tracker from additional automation, analysis and exclusive content. Core training can be done without paying. Pro is most relevant when you want its extra tools to change a specific planning or review task.

## How should you choose a program without getting lost in the library?

Start with constraints before selecting a famous coach. A five-day plan is not a better option if you reliably train three days. Check whether the exercises use equipment you have, how long sessions are likely to take and what the progression expects you to record.

Use this shortlist before importing a program:

- Days per week you can sustain, including busy weeks.
- Experience level and familiarity with the required movements.
- Available equipment and acceptable substitutions.
- How the plan changes load, repetitions or sets.
- What to do after a missed session or missed target.

Then commit to understanding one plan. If you change several movements, training days and progression rules immediately, you are evaluating your modified version. That may be appropriate, but it should be written down rather than attributed entirely to the original program.

## What does the Muscle Tracker actually tell you?

Boostcamp describes its Pro Muscle Tracker as a view of per-muscle training volume. The [feature overview](https://www.boostcamp.app/features) and Pro page describe heatmaps and analysis derived from logged work. Those records can show where your program allocates attention; they do not directly measure muscle size or prove that a particular area grew.

The practical question is whether the distribution matches your plan. If you intended to prioritize pulling but logged mostly pressing, the record exposes a mismatch you can investigate. It does not establish the ideal amount of training for every person, and the label attached to a muscle is not a diagnosis of a weak body part.

Compare performance within a movement under similar conditions. An estimated one-rep maximum is useful as an estimate; a different rep range, technique or effort changes the comparison. If you want another logging format, our [Hevy versus Strong guide](/blog/hevy-vs-strong/) covers that choice.

## Does Boostcamp support photos and private workouts?

Yes. The [Community and sharing guide](https://www.boostcamp.app/features/community) describes workout photo cards, sharing destinations and per-workout visibility. It lists Everyone, Followers and Only Me, with Everyone shown as the default. Review your default before adding a photo you only intend to keep for yourself.

A saved workout photo can be a useful memory or accountability record. A repeated-pose body check-in has a different purpose. You can do both, but keep a relaxed baseline photo separate from a celebratory post-workout image if you want to compare appearance across months.

## When should you choose a simpler log instead?

If a coach already gives you every session and your current log works, a large program library may add little. If you enjoy creating your own plans, judge Boostcamp's builder against your existing process rather than subscribing for features you will not use. If discovering a plan is the missing step, the free library is a sensible place to begin.

The broader [workout tracker comparison](/blog/best-workout-tracker-apps/) can help you separate programming, logging and social needs. The right app is the one that makes your chosen plan easier to follow and review, not the one with the most impressive number of templates.

## What else should you know before choosing?

### Is Boostcamp free to use?

<p>Boostcamp provides a free workout tracker and a substantial program library. Its current Pro page reserves additional automation, analysis and selected programs for paid access. Try the free workflow with the program you actually want to run. Confirm any specific paid feature in the app rather than assuming every advertised tool belongs to the free tier.</p>

### Is Boostcamp Pro worth it?

<p>Pro is most relevant when its automation or analysis removes a task you currently do manually, such as adjusting progression or reviewing training distribution. A beginner following one suitable free program may not need it immediately. During a trial, identify the feature you would miss and check the renewal price before committing.</p>

### Does Boostcamp have progress photos?

<p>Boostcamp supports workout photos and share cards through its Community features. Those can document sessions and milestones, with visibility controls for who sees the workout. A standardized body-photo series requires consistent pose, framing and lighting regardless of where it is stored. Do not confuse a shared workout image with an independent measurement of body composition.</p>

### Can I keep Boostcamp workouts private?

<p>The official Community guide lists Everyone, Followers and Only Me visibility options. It describes Everyone as the default, so review that setting before your first shared workout. Only Me keeps the workout off the feed. If you choose to post a photo externally, review that destination separately because its privacy settings are independent.</p>

### Does Boostcamp measure how much muscle I gained?

<p>Its workout analysis describes the training you logged, including volume, records and muscle-group allocation. That is different from directly measuring muscle tissue. Use consistent performance records alongside dated photos and any measurements you normally collect. A higher volume total or a colored muscle map alone cannot establish a precise amount of muscle gained.</p>
