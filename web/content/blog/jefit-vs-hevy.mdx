<div className="post-callout post-quick-answer"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Hevy suits lifters who want straightforward workout logging, shared routines, and private progress photos. Jefit suits people who value its exercise and routine libraries. Compare current free limits and device support before choosing. Both record training and body data; photo analysis is a separate capability, with its own estimation limits.</p></div>

You have a four-day split to save and months of old lifts to keep. Both Jefit and Hevy let you record sets, reps, and the changes you are working toward. The useful question is which one fits your actual routine: finding exercise instructions, repeating a saved session, checking a previous lift, or sharing a workout with a friend.

This comparison uses current product documentation and US App Store listings, checked October 9, 2026. It does not claim a timed usability test or an independent ranking of every exercise database. For the other common logging comparison, see [Hevy vs Strong](/blog/hevy-vs-strong/).

## What does Jefit offer for workout planning?

[Jefit's current iOS listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jefit-workout-plan-gym-tracker/id449810000) advertises more than 1,400 exercises with video or animation instruction. It also lists custom workouts, supersets, rest timers, set types, and ready-made bodybuilding programs. That gives you several ways to start a session without designing every movement from scratch.

The listing also describes adaptive plans and recommendations for weights and reps, with premium training tools under Elite. If exercise instruction and a program library are your priorities, check that your equipment and preferred split are represented before subscribing.

Jefit records more than workout volume. Its listing includes body weight, entered body fat and circumference measurements, and progress photos attached to workouts. Those records can sit alongside your estimated one-rep max and training charts.

## What does Hevy offer for everyday logging?

[Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/) combines workout logging with shared routines and a social feed. You can use those social features for training partners or keep your profile private. Its exercise library includes demonstrations, and custom exercises let you add movements missing from the catalog.

The [current Hevy feature guide](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/33106320824727-Everything-You-Need-to-Know-About-the-Hevy-App-2026-Features-Guide) lists four saved routines and seven custom exercises on the free tier. Pro expands those limits and adds longer statistics history, additional measurements, and Hevy Trainer's guided programming. Both free and Pro support Apple Watch and Wear OS workouts.

That makes a practical trial straightforward: enter one normal training week and see whether your routine count, equipment, and preferred watch workflow fit. A four-day split and a rotating six-session plan can encounter different limits even when both involve four gym visits per week.

## What should you check about price and ads?

Jefit's US iOS description lists Elite at **$12.99 monthly or $69.99 annually** on the date checked. Hevy offers monthly, annual, and lifetime Pro options through its [subscription flow](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35119778922263-Hevy-Pro-Subscription-How-to-get-Pro-and-What-Does-It-Include). Confirm the checkout price and included features on your device, since storefronts and offers can differ.

Jefit's own pages give conflicting signals about ads: its [workout-app page](https://www.jefit.com/use-case/best-workout-app) says free to start with no ads, while the FAQ on that same page advertises an ad-free experience as an Elite benefit. That evidence does not support a blanket claim that every current free user sees ads. Check the version and tier you intend to use.

Hevy's [iOS listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hevy-workout-tracker-gym-log/id1458862350) describes the app as ad-free. Its Pro upsell and feature limits are still relevant to the choice. Try the free logging workflow before deciding which paid extras you need.

## How do photos and body measurements fit alongside workout logs?

Both apps document progress photos and body records. Hevy's [measurement and photo guide](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35385479603479-Body-Composition-Tracking-Measurements-and-Progress-Photos) specifically describes private photos, an overlay of the previous photo for alignment, and a comparison view. Photos remain private unless you choose to share them in a post.

Entered body fat values and circumference measurements are records you supply. Saving or comparing photos is a separate function from estimating body composition from an image. The cited documentation does not establish an AI photo-based body fat estimate for either app.

## Can you move your history from Jefit to Hevy?

Hevy's [import documentation](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/38001424401943-How-to-Import-Strong-App-CSV-Files-and-Export-Your-Data-in-Hevy) supports Strong CSV files in English with kilograms. It says other app exports would need adapting to that format and does not offer support for those conversions. A CSV extension alone does not mean a Jefit export will import correctly.

Keep an original copy of any available export, and test a small converted sample before relying on a migration. Check dates, exercise names, units, and set counts against the original log. Keep access to your old history until you have verified the records you need.

## Which app should you try first?

**Try Jefit first** if a large exercise-instruction library and ready-made programs are central to your training. Check a few exercises you actually perform and inspect the plan you would follow.

**Try Hevy first** if shared routines, private photo comparisons, and its documented watch options fit your week. Confirm whether four free saved routines cover your split or whether you need Pro.

For either app, use one normal week as the trial:

- Save the routines you actually follow.
- Record a session and change an exercise.
- Review a previous workout on your preferred device.
- Add the measurements or photos you want to keep.

## What else should you know?

### Is Jefit or Hevy better?

<p>Hevy is a reasonable starting point for shared routines, straightforward logging, and private photo comparisons. Jefit is worth trying for its exercise instructions and program library. Choose using your own routine count, devices, and preferred workflow. App Store ratings are useful context, but they cannot determine which app fits your training.</p>

### Is Jefit free?

<p>Jefit is free to download and provides core workout logging, with an optional Elite subscription for premium tools. Its US iOS listing showed $12.99 monthly or $69.99 annually when checked on October 9, 2026. Confirm the current checkout price and included features for your storefront before paying.</p>

### Does Jefit have ads?

<p>Jefit's current public pages conflict: one says free to start with no ads, while its FAQ lists an ad-free experience among Elite benefits. That makes a universal claim about the free tier unreliable. Check the installed version and plan you intend to use; Hevy's iOS listing describes its app as ad-free.</p>

### Can I switch from Jefit to Hevy without losing my data?

<p>Do not assume a direct transfer is supported. Hevy documents imports from Strong CSV files in English with kilograms. Other exports would need conversion to that format, which Hevy does not support. Keep your original history, test any conversion carefully, and verify exercise names, dates, units, and sets before relying on it.</p>

### Do Jefit or Hevy track body composition or progress photos?

<p>Both apps document progress photos and entered body measurements. Hevy includes private photos, a previous-photo overlay, and a comparison view; Pro expands the measurements available. Jefit lists body weight, body fat, circumferences, and workout photos. These recording features should be distinguished from AI photo analysis, which produces estimates with separate limitations.</p>
