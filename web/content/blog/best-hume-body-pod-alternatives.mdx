Shopping for a replacement can become an expensive search for a number you prefer. The best Hume Body Pod alternatives depend on which part of the experience you actually want to change.

This guide compares four different jobs: simpler automatic weigh-ins, staying inside Garmin, retaining hand-and-foot BIA hardware, and tracking visual changes without another scale. It is a sourced selection checked October 27, 2026, not a laboratory accuracy ranking or a hands-on device test. Product images come from manufacturers and the app publisher.

Start by naming the problem. If syncing is the friction, evaluate the app connection. If the recurring cost bothers you, identify which features require payment. If a body-fat estimate changes between readings, another estimator may still be sensitive to conditions. A photo record answers a different question and should be evaluated separately.

## Is [Withings Body Smart](https://www.withings.com/en-us/collections/body-smart) the right choice for simpler daily weigh-ins?

**Platform:** Scale with the Withings app on [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/withings/id542701020) and Android. **Listed US hardware price:** $129.95, checked October 27. **Companion-app US rating:** 4.58/5 from 368,638 US ratings. That rating covers the app, not Body Smart accuracy.

<div className="post-render-figure scroll-reveal"><img src="assets/withings-body-smart.webp" alt="Withings Body Smart scale with its body-fat display on the glass platform" loading="lazy" /><p className="post-caption">Official product image of Body Smart. This model has no hand-held electrode bar.</p></div>

Withings lists body-composition estimates, Wi-Fi synchronization and automatic recognition for up to eight users. Its current [Body Smart page](https://www.withings.com/en-us/collections/body-smart) says baseline metrics and trends remain free, while Withings+ is optional. That makes it a sensible shortlist entry if your priority is automatic records without paying for the premium guidance layer.

The tradeoff is method and scope. Body Smart is a foot-contact scale; it is not the handled Body Scan or BodyScan 2. Do not transfer the more expensive models' segmental or cardiac features onto it. The [official Body Smart FAQ](https://support.withings.com/hc/en-us/articles/16134680325777-Body-Smart-FAQ) describes that distinction.

**Choose it if:** you want uncomplicated weigh-ins and an established connected-device ecosystem. **Reconsider if:** your reason for replacing Hume is specifically to retain a hand-and-foot setup. A lower price and fewer steps do not establish superior individual body-fat accuracy.

## Is [Garmin Index S2](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/679362/) better if you already use Garmin?

**Platform:** Scale with Garmin Connect on [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/garmin-connect/id583446403) and Android. **Hardware cost:** check the current Garmin checkout; the launch price is not a verified current offer. **Companion-app US rating:** 4.39/5 from 59,218 US ratings.

<div className="post-render-figure scroll-reveal"><img src="assets/garmin-index-s2.webp" alt="Black Garmin Index S2 scale displaying body weight" loading="lazy" /><p className="post-caption">Official Garmin Index S2 product image. The screen shown is a weight reading, not an accuracy test.</p></div>

Index S2 is most interesting when your training already lives in Garmin Connect. Garmin documents Wi-Fi uploads and a connected history in its [product announcement](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/newsroom/press-release/sports-fitness/2020-measure-more-with-the-index-s2-smart-scale-from-garmin/). The reason to consider it is fewer places to look, not a claim that a shared dashboard creates better measurements.

The [current manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0BADEBCF-960D-4961-856C-86B9204BC169/EN-GB/GUID-CB348359-1E39-491B-86B5-79CB12FB6A8C.html) explains that it estimates composition from a foot-to-foot electrical signal and that timing, hydration and recent activities can affect readings. Its body-fat, water and muscle outputs should therefore be interpreted as estimates from that device.

**Choose it if:** the Garmin connection would make you review your records more consistently. **Reconsider if:** you are buying it solely to settle a disagreement between two body-fat percentages. A third estimate is not automatically the tie-breaker.

**Platform:** Home scale with the InBody app on [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/inbody/id884923678) and Android. **Listed US hardware price:** $341.10 sale / $379 regular, checked October 27. **Companion-app US rating:** 3.95/5 from 1,909 ratings. That is an app rating, not a validation score for the H30.

The US manufacturer page specifies multi-frequency BIA with hand and foot electrodes, Bluetooth and Wi-Fi app syncing, and outputs including body-fat percentage and skeletal-muscle mass. That makes H30 a more directly comparable hardware category for someone who specifically wants a handled home BIA device.

Do not read “InBody” as a guarantee that this home model reproduces a particular gym machine. Different models and protocols need their own evidence. The manufacturer's precision claims are product claims; we have not established an individual error advantage over Hume in a controlled comparison.

**Choose it if:** retaining a hand-and-foot measurement routine is important enough to justify the purchase price. **Reconsider if:** your main need is simply to see whether your waist and photos change across a training block. You may be buying a more elaborate measurement routine than you need.

## How do you switch without turning a device change into fake progress?

Keep the old and new measurement series distinguishable. If one reads 20% and the other 24% on the same day, that is a disagreement between methods or devices, not evidence you gained four percentage points of fat during setup. Record the date, model and method whenever the source changes.

A short overlap can reveal an offset, but it does not establish which device is accurate. Use each manufacturer's measurement protocol, inspect trends under similar conditions and keep your training and photo records alongside them. For a broader method decision, see [alternatives to DEXA and their limits](/blog/dexa-scan-alternative/).

- Name the specific problem you want the replacement to solve.
- Compare the exact model, not only the manufacturer.
- Check the free reports and paid features before buying hardware.
- Save or export the old record before changing accounts or devices.
- Start a clearly dated baseline for the new method.

There is no universal winner here. Body Smart emphasizes simpler connected weigh-ins, Index S2 emphasizes ecosystem continuity, H30 retains a handled BIA routine, and a dedicated photo workflow covers visible change. Choose the job first, then the product.

## What else should you know before choosing?

### What is the best alternative to the Hume Body Pod?

<p>Choose according to the problem you want to solve. Body Smart is a simpler connected-scale option, Index S2 fits the Garmin ecosystem, and Dial H30 retains hand-and-foot BIA hardware. Photo tracking is useful for visible changes. None is automatically the most accurate for every person, and these choices do not perform identical jobs.</p>

### Is a cheaper scale necessarily less accurate than Hume?

<p>Price alone does not establish individual body-fat accuracy. Compare the measurement method, exact model and relevant validation evidence rather than assuming the more expensive device must be right. Weight precision and body-composition accuracy are also different claims. A scale can report weight consistently while its derived composition estimates remain sensitive to conditions.</p>

### Can a photo app replace a body-composition scale?

<p>A photo app can replace the job of organizing visible progress, but it does not replace weighing yourself or taking an impedance reading. Image-based body-composition outputs are estimates with their own limitations. Keep the method labeled and choose it because the visual record is useful, not because it is clinically equivalent to a scale or scan.</p>

### Does Withings Body Smart require Withings+?

<p>The current Body Smart page says baseline metrics and trends remain available in the free Withings app. Withings+ is an optional premium service. Check which particular guidance or reports you want before purchasing a bundle, and read any trial-renewal terms. The existence of optional paid features does not make the basic scale subscription-only.</p>

### Why do two body-composition scales give different results?

<p>Each device turns an electrical signal and other inputs into estimates using its own method and model. Measurement conditions can also differ. A disagreement does not tell you which reading is correct, and averaging two results does not validate them. Keep each series identifiable and avoid interpreting a switch of device as an immediate change in your body.</p>
