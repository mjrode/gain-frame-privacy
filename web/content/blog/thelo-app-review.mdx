Most fitness apps make you choose a camp: workout planners that know your sets and nothing about your body, or body trackers that know your body and nothing about your training. Thelo's bet is that the scan should drive the plan — point the camera at yourself, and the workouts reshape around what it sees.

That dual-camp design is why it took the #2 spot in our [AI fitness apps that track your body roundup](/blog/best-ai-fitness-apps-track-body/). This review goes deeper on Thelo itself: what the BioScan actually feeds into, what the subscription costs, and where the honest gaps are.

## What does the Thelo app actually do?

Thelo (from One Labs LLC, launched January 2025) runs on a loop: scan, plan, train, rescan. The BioScan reads your body through the phone camera and, per the app's own description, identifies muscle imbalances and weak points, structure and proportions, and which muscles are recovered. The planner then builds sessions around your schedule, equipment, and training history, and keeps adjusting as new scans come in.

Three things it does genuinely well:

-   **Scan-to-plan integration.** This is the feature that earns the download. Most stacks make you manually translate body data into routine changes; Thelo does the translation automatically. Cut too hard and lose lean mass, and the plan responds.
-   **Adaptive scheduling.** Sessions adjust to available time and equipment rather than assuming a fixed gym week — a real advantage for people whose training windows move around.
-   **Active development.** Version 2.2.4 shipped in late July 2026, and the 4.6 rating across ~1,300 App Store ratings is a solid signal for an app this young.

## Where is Thelo thin?

The biggest gap is verification. Thelo describes the BioScan as "proprietary body scan technology" and publishes no validation data — no DEXA comparison, no error range, no methodology beyond the marketing copy. The scan may well be consistent enough for trend tracking; there's simply no public evidence either way, which matters when the whole product is built on top of the scan's output.

The second gap is lock-in. The plan is Thelo's plan. If you already run Fitbod, follow a coach's program, or write your own training, the planner half of the app is redundant and you're paying for a scanner with a workout app attached. The scan output is also less granular than a per-muscle-group breakdown — you get directional insights rather than a scored report per body part.

Worth knowing as a buyer: the app has been renamed repeatedly — it launched as Swoosh, became "Thelo: Body Scan & Weights AI," and is now "Thelo: AI Personal Trainer." Legacy IAP names from the Swoosh era are still visible in the listing. None of that says anything about the product's quality; it does suggest the positioning is still settling.

## Who should use Thelo?

**Use Thelo if:** you want one app for everything, you don't have a program you're attached to, and the idea of workouts that reshape around your scan results is the feature you'd pay for. Nothing else in the category integrates the two halves this directly.

**Skip Thelo if:** you already have programming you trust (the planner becomes dead weight), you need validated accuracy numbers before trusting a scan, or you want per-muscle scoring rather than directional insights. ### What is the Thelo app?

If you already train with Fitbod, a coach, or your own program, the planner side becomes redundant and the subscription is harder to justify.

Treat its numbers as trend data — useful for tracking direction over time rather than as clinical measurements.
