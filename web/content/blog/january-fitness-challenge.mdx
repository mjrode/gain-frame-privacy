<div className="post-callout post-quick-answer"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Print the January fitness challenge calendar and put it where you choose your shoes each morning. Each week has two short strength sessions, three walks and two rest days. Start on whichever date you have available. On January 31, repeat one familiar walk and compare how it felt.</p></div>

January 1, 2027 falls on a Friday. If your gym is closed, the first square still works: pick a familiar route, walk for ten minutes and write down where you turned around. On January 31 you can repeat that route even if you missed a week in the middle.

**[Download the one-page January 2027 fitness calendar](assets/january-2027-calendar.pdf)**. It prints on US Letter paper and scales to A4. Each date has a checkbox. The bottom margin has space for your route and the exercise you want to repeat.

## What goes on the January fitness challenge calendar?

| Day | Plan | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Easy walk for 10 minutes | Route or turnaround point |
| Tuesday | Strength A for up to 15 minutes | Reps on one movement |
| Wednesday | Rest | Leave the square as printed |
| Thursday | Easy walk for 10 minutes | How the walk felt |
| Friday | Strength B for up to 15 minutes | Reps on one movement |
| Saturday | Walk for 10 to 20 minutes | Route or time |
| Sunday | Rest | Pick a time for Monday |

January 1 and 31 are the two exceptions to that weekly pattern. Both are ten-minute walks on your chosen route. You can do the whole calendar without joining a gym. A hallway or an indoor track works if sidewalks are icy.

## What do the two short strength sessions look like?

For **A**, use a stable chair for a sit-to-stand and a clear wall for a standing push-up. Try five comfortable repetitions of each. Rest until you feel ready. Repeat that pair if you have time and it still feels comfortable. Write something specific in the margin, such as “five wall push-ups, feet one shoe length from wall.”

For **B**, choose a familiar lower-body movement and a familiar pulling movement. At a gym that might be a leg press and a seated row. At home you can repeat the chair stands and use a band row if you already have a suitable setup. If you have no pulling equipment, repeat session A. Use the same setup the following week so the entries are comparable.

## What happens if you miss a day?

Suppose you last checked a box on Tuesday the 12th and return Saturday the 16th. Do Saturday’s walk. Circle the date you came back and leave the three missed boxes empty. There is no extra Friday session to squeeze in before dinner. If Tuesdays are always awkward, swap Tuesday and Wednesday on your own copy.

## How can you compare the first and last days?

Write the time, route and your impression of the walk. On the last day, use the same route and roughly the same pace. “I kept walking up the hill where I paused on January 1” will tell you more about that particular walk than two photos taken in different rooms.

If you want photos too, mark a spot for the phone and another for your feet. Use a timer, the same clothing and the same framing. ## What else should you know?

### Can I start after January 1?

<p>Start on the date you have. If your first day is January 11, use that Monday walk as your baseline and write the route in the margin. You can repeat it on January 31. Leave the earlier squares blank so your calendar reflects the days you actually did.</p>

### Do I need to walk outdoors?

<p>An indoor route works too. Use a stretch of hallway, a mall loop or a treadmill you know how to operate. Write down the route or the treadmill setting on the first day so that you have a specific reference when you repeat the walk.</p>

### Should I increase the weights every Tuesday?

<p>You can use the same resistance throughout the month. Record how many comfortable reps you completed with the same setup. If you already follow a lifting program, keep its progression rules and count those sessions in the calendar instead of doing two additional workouts.</p>

### How should I mark rest days?

<p>Leave the word Rest visible and check the square if that helps you follow the plan. A scheduled rest day counts as following your calendar. If a longer walk or a regular training session needs a different day, move the rest square along with it.</p>

### Do I have to take a before photo?

<p>The calendar works with a route and a few written notes. If photos would make the month less enjoyable, skip them. On the last day you can still compare two walks, two sets of chair stands or simply the number of weeks you returned to your routine.</p>
