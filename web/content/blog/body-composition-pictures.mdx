## Why do two people at the same number look different?

Because the percentage describes a ratio, and the body decides the layout. Muscle is the biggest factor: a muscular person at 20% shows shape everywhere, while an untrained person at the same 20% simply looks soft — the [skinny-fat pattern](/blog/am-i-skinny-fat-quiz/) in a single comparison.

Distribution is the second factor, and it's genetic. Some people store centrally, some in the hips and thighs; men and women differ systematically, and [age shifts the pattern too](/blog/average-body-fat-percentage-by-age/). Height and frame width do the rest — the same pounds of fat spread differently across a 5'6" and a 6'2" skeleton.

So use the pictures as a band, and expect your version of any level to be personal. The renders control for everything except the one thing no reference image can: being you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are body composition pictures accurate for judging yourself?

They give you a band, and that's their honest limit. Visual comparison against standardized images typically narrows you to within several percentage points, which is enough to pick a training direction. Lighting, pump, water, and camera angle all move how you photograph, so compare a relaxed, consistent photo of yourself rather than a flexed gym-lighting shot.

### Why do two people at the same body fat percentage look different?

Muscle mass, fat distribution, height, and frame width all change how a percentage presents. A muscular person at 20% shows shape everywhere; an untrained person at 20% just looks soft. Distribution is genetic too — some store centrally, others in the hips and thighs. The percentage describes the ratio, and the body decides the layout.

### What is a healthy body composition?

Reference ranges commonly describe roughly 10–20% body fat as fit-to-healthy for men and 18–28% for women, with athletic ranges below that and elevated risk above roughly 25% for men and 32% for women. Muscle mass matters alongside fat — two healthy compositions can hold very different amounts of both.

### How do I find out my own body composition?

Ranked roughly by accuracy: a DEXA scan measures it directly for around $100–150; smart scales estimate it at home with wide error bars; and AI photo tools estimate it free from a standardized picture. For tracking change over months, a consistent method matters more than the most accurate one — the trend survives a constant error.
