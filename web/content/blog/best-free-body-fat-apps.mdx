## What does "free" actually mean in this category?

Four kinds of free, and they behave nothing alike:

**Free tier with a cap.** You get a fixed allowance and then the app asks for money. Honest, because you know the number up front.

**Free trial, then paywall.** You get days, not units, and a card on file. This is the pattern behind most "free" App Store listings in this niche.

**Free with ads.** No money, plenty of attention. Usually paired with a one-shot result and no history.

**Genuinely free forever.** Tape measures and formulas. No compute cost behind them, so nothing to charge for.

**Platform:** any browser · **Actually free:** 3 estimates, one per day, no account

**Best for:** finding out roughly where you are today with zero commitment. **Limitations:** three scans total, no history, single-photo error range.

### 3. MeThreeSixty — free scanning on both platforms, five-scan memory

**Platform:** iOS & Android · **Actually free:** unlimited scans, but only your five most recent are kept

MeThreeSixty comes from Size Stream, a company that builds professional scanning hardware, and it turns two poses into a 3D avatar with 14+ circumference estimates plus a body fat figure. Scanning is free on both platforms and processes on-device, which is a genuinely strong free offer.

The catch is memory rather than volume. Recent versions keep only your five most recent scans on the free tier, with longer history moving to Premium at around $5 a month. For a monthly check that's fine; for a year-long record it isn't.

**Best for:** the strongest free option on Android, and free circumference numbers. **Limitations:** five-scan history cap; a smoothed avatar shows shape rather than definition.

### 4. The Navy tape method — the one that's free forever

**Platform:** a soft tape measure · **Actually free:** completely and permanently

Men measure neck and waist, women measure neck, waist, and hips, then feed those plus height into a logarithmic formula. Done with good technique it's commonly cited as landing within roughly 3–4 percentage points of DEXA, which is competitive with anything AI-powered on this page.

Nothing about it expires, upsells, or needs a network connection. Our [how to measure body fat with a tape](/blog/how-to-measure-body-fat-with-tape/) guide has the formulas, the measurement points, and the technique errors that wreck the result.

**Best for:** anyone who wants a repeatable number with no software involved. **Limitations:** it reads two or three circumferences, so it's blind to muscle distribution; measurement technique is the whole ballgame.

### 5. Renpho and other BIA scale apps — free software, paid hardware

**Platform:** iOS & Android app, plus a scale · **Actually free:** the entire app; the scale costs around $30

The app half of the BIA ecosystem is free and generally good — trend charts, multi-user support, Apple Health sync, no subscription for the core metrics. The cost is hardware, usually $20–35 for a Renpho-class scale.

Then there's the number itself. Foot-to-foot bioelectrical impedance is heavily affected by hydration, meal timing, and time of day, commonly moving 2–4 points inside a week when your actual body fat barely changed. Our [Renpho scale review](/blog/renpho-scale-review/) works through what that $30 buys and which number to trust.

**Best for:** automated daily weight with a body fat estimate attached. **Limitations:** hydration noise; the free part requires buying hardware first.

### 6. Free web analyzers — the zero-install lane

**Platform:** any browser · **Actually free:** usually one session, sometimes with a soft cap

Browser-based photo analyzers sidestep the platform question entirely — they work on Android, on a laptop, on anything with a camera roll. LeanLens is the best known and is upfront that its estimates are directional rather than medical, which is the right framing for this whole category. Our [LeanLens alternatives roundup](/blog/best-leanlens-alternatives/) compares the field.

The useful trick with free web tools is calibration. Run the same photo through two independent analyzers — if they land within a couple of points of each other, the neighborhood is probably right; if they're eight points apart, stop trusting the absolute number and watch the trend.

**Best for:** Android users and anyone who won't install anything. **Limitations:** most keep no history, and free limits change without notice.

### 7. Ad-supported one-shot raters — read this before you install one

**Platform:** iOS and Android · **Actually free:** the number, paid for with ads and sometimes your photo

There's a steady supply of body fat apps that are free in the sense that no money changes hands. Two things worth checking before you upload a body photo to one.

First, methodology. Accuracy claims in the 90s with no published validation are marketing copy, and several of these apps quote a precision they have no way to support. Second, what happens to the image. Some of these tools upload to a server and describe it as "processed securely," which is not the same sentence as "not retained." Read the privacy policy, not the App Store screenshot.

**Best for:** essentially nothing you can't get more safely above. **Limitations:** unclear methodology, unclear photo handling, no tracking.

## What should you expect from a free body fat estimate?

A number with a range around it, and more value in the second reading than the first.

Every method on this page produces an estimate. Photo AI commonly sits around 4–5 points of absolute error from a single image, the Navy tape method around 3–4 with clean technique, and BIA swings 2–4 points week to week on hydration alone. Paying more doesn't reliably close those gaps.

What does close the gap is repetition under identical conditions. Same time of day, same lighting, same clothing, same tape placement. The absolute number stays fuzzy; the direction gets sharp fast. Reference ranges to interpret whatever you get: men commonly land at 6–13% athletic, 14–17% fitness, 18–24% average, 25%+ obese; women at 14–20%, 21–24%, 25–31%, and 32%+ respectively.

## How do you build a free tracking setup that lasts?

<div className="post-steps">

1. **Get a baseline from two methods.** A free photo estimate plus a tape measurement. Agreement means you can trust the neighborhood.
2. **Pick the one you'll actually repeat.** The tape wins on cost, the photo wins on effort. Consistency beats precision either way.
3. **Standardize the conditions.** Morning, fasted, same lighting, same clothing. Most of the noise people blame on the app comes from here.
4. **Re-measure every 2–4 weeks.** Body fat moves roughly half a point to a point per month in a deficit, so anything more frequent is mostly hydration.
5. **Spend your free allowance on the interval that matters.** Twenty-five free photos across six months tells you more than 25 across three weeks.
