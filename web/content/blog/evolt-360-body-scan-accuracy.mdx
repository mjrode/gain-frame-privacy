Two gym reports disagree, and both print body fat to one decimal place. The decimal makes the result look settled. Before changing your plan, work out whether the disagreement comes from the measurement method, the setup, or a real change you have enough evidence to interpret.

**Research basis:** This guide reviews Evolt's published methodology and explains how to evaluate a report. We have not performed an Evolt scan or collected repeatability data. Sources were checked September 6, 2026. The author is Michael Rode, founder of GainFrame, which offers photo-based tracking.

## What does Evolt 360 measure directly?

Evolt describes a hand-and-foot bioelectrical impedance system. Its published methodology lists age, gender, height, weight, and impedance as inputs to its algorithm. That means the report combines observations and supplied profile information with calculated estimates. [Evolt's accuracy explanation](https://evolt.health/article/facts-about-the-evolt-360-body-scan-accuracy)

The electrical measurement is not a photograph of your muscle or fat tissue. A reported composition value depends on how the system translates its inputs. A change in an input or an algorithm can therefore affect more than one output.

| Part of the process | What to verify on your report |
|---|---|
| Profile inputs | Height, age, units, and the profile selected by the operator |
| Weight | Whether the reading is plausible and the setup was consistent |
| Impedance assessment | Whether contact and positioning followed the instructions |
| Estimated composition | Which exact metric, unit, and algorithm the number represents |
| Comparison with an earlier scan | Whether both records came from the same system and procedure |

If your question is about BIA versus DXA as methods, the existing [InBody versus DEXA guide](/blog/inbody-vs-dexa/) covers that broader comparison. Here the task is evaluating an Evolt result.

## What would a convincing accuracy claim include?

A useful validation report identifies the actual device and software, participants, reference method, and error distribution. It should tell you whether the result concerns total body fat, regional estimates, weight, or another output.

A statement about correlation is insufficient on its own. Two methods can rank people similarly while returning systematically different values. Likewise, a claim about repeatability describes how close repeated results are under specified conditions; it does not establish agreement with a reference assessment.

The Evolt marketing page we reviewed explains the approach but does not provide enough individual-level validation detail to assign a dependable error range to your scan. Further research may exist beyond the material reviewed here.

## Why can two Evolt reports disagree?

Start by auditing the comparison before interpreting it. Ask the operator whether the same device, profile, scan procedure, and software were used. Record recent food, drink, exercise, and unusual circumstances so the operator has context when assessing a difference.

Follow the provider's preparation instructions rather than deliberately dehydrating or changing your normal medication routine to chase a lower estimate. Evolt's [how-to-use page](https://evolt.health/how-to-use) is a starting point; the staff running your appointment should clarify the current procedure and eligibility.

A report's consistency also does not make separate outputs independent. If body-fat percentage, fat mass, and lean mass all shift, check whether they are mathematically connected before treating that as three pieces of evidence.

## What should you record between two appointments?

Use this worksheet with your operator. It is an empty comparison framework and contains no patient or test results.

| Comparison field | Earlier appointment | Later appointment |
|---|---|---|
| Date, location, and device | Record | Record |
| Profile and units checked | Record | Record |
| Preparation and recent exercise | Record | Record |
| Body-fat estimate | Record | Record |
| Absolute fat and lean estimates | Record | Record |
| Waist landmark and reading | Record | Record |
| Training and photo notes | Record | Record |
| Operator's explanation of uncertainty | Record | Record |

Keep the report itself, including its labels. Avoid copying only the headline score into a note and losing the information needed to compare it later. If an output changes its name or units, resolve that before calculating a difference.

## How should you respond to a surprising muscle result?

Ask whether the field represents lean mass, skeletal muscle, a percentage, or a proprietary score. These labels are not interchangeable. Then review whether the result agrees with a longer record of training and other observations.

A hypothetical example makes the problem clear: one scan reports less estimated lean mass after an unusually different preparation day. That alone does not establish that your training caused muscle loss. You need a comparable assessment and relevant context before drawing that conclusion.

Keep [consistent progress photos](/tools/progress-photo-setup/) if visual change is one of your goals. They add a record of appearance, but they cannot validate an Evolt percentage or diagnose muscle loss.

## What else should you know?

### How accurate is an Evolt 360 body scan?

This guide cannot assign an individual error range from the public methodology page alone. Evolt uses impedance and personal inputs to estimate composition. To evaluate an accuracy claim, ask for validation of the specific device, algorithm, and output against a stated reference method, including the spread of individual errors.

### Does a repeatable Evolt result mean it is accurate?

Repeatability means repeated results are close under the conditions tested. Accuracy concerns agreement with the value or reference method being assessed. A system can return similar estimates repeatedly while differing from another assessment. Ask which property a quoted statistic measures before applying it to your body-fat result.

### Why did my Evolt muscle estimate change?

First confirm the field name, units, profile, procedure, and whether the appointments are comparable. Estimated lean mass and skeletal muscle are different outputs. A surprising difference deserves discussion with the operator and review alongside your longer training record; a single change cannot establish how much muscle tissue you gained or lost.

### Can you compare Evolt with a different scanner?

You can record both results, but do not splice them into one continuous trend as though they use the same measurement model. Label each device and method. If you change providers, establish a new baseline and keep the older report for context rather than treating the difference as a physical change.

### What should you track between Evolt scans?

Keep the dated reports, scan conditions, profile details, and a consistent record of the observations relevant to your goal. That might include training performance, waist measurements, and progress photos. The purpose is to give the next assessment context, not to turn every daily fluctuation into a new body-composition conclusion.
