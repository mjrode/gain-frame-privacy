## How should you compare accuracy claims?

Look for the exact app version, tested population, sample size and individual differences against a reference. A single close match is useful as an example but cannot establish expected error for everyone. A study of one imaging method also cannot validate every app that uses the word AI.

Keep lighting, pose, camera distance and clothing consistent. If two apps disagree, avoid averaging their numbers into a supposedly better answer. Retain the separate series and inspect the underlying images. Our [measurement-method guide](/blog/every-way-to-measure-body-fat/) explains the different kinds of evidence.

## What should you check before your first scan?

- Confirm that the app is available in your country's Store.
- Check whether analysis, history and export require payment.
- Read what happens to uploaded images and what deletion controls exist.
- Choose one repeatable capture setup.
- Save your baseline and compare later check-ins under similar conditions.

Choose the output first, inspect the purchase screen second, then try the capture process before committing to a longer subscription.

### Which body-fat app should I choose for progress tracking?

<p>Choose the app whose record you will actually revisit. A photo timeline suits visual comparisons, a composition report suits reviewing estimated metrics, and a 3D model suits shape tracking. Check history access, export options and subscription requirements before deciding that the first attractive result is enough.</p>

### How should I read the 36-photo DEXA benchmark?

<p>The published internal benchmark used 36 clean front-view photos from a female dataset paired with DEXA results. It describes that test configuration and sample. It does not establish universal accuracy across sexes, poses, devices or app versions, and it does not prove superiority over every other app.</p>

### Can a phone app measure body fat directly?

<p>A phone app estimates body composition from images and any information you provide. It does not directly observe every tissue in your body. Keep the original images and standardize capture conditions so you can inspect visible changes alongside the estimates, rather than relying on one precise-looking number.</p>

### Does a high App Store rating prove accuracy?

<p>No. A Store rating reflects reviews from customers who chose to rate the app. It does not compare those customers with a reference measurement under a controlled protocol. Use ratings to investigate usability and support, then evaluate accuracy evidence separately for the particular app and method.</p>

### Is a free download the same as free analysis?

<p>No. An app can be free to install while charging for a report, history or advanced analysis. Check the current purchase screen before subscribing. In this comparison, trackBod explicitly requires a subscription for analysis, while other apps describe different combinations of free and paid features.</p>
