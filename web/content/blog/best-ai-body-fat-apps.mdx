## 1. Is GainFrame the best AI body fat app for tracking progress?

**Platform:** [iPhone and iPad](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gainframe-gym-progress-photos/id6759252082) | **Price:** Free tier; Pro at $5.99/month or $39.99/year | **US rating:** 4.96 from 51 ratings

[GainFrame](https://gainframe.app) wins this comparison for the person who plans to check again next month. A check-in returns body fat percentage, BMI, FFMI, a 1 to 100 GainFrame Score, and ratings for 12 muscle groups. The app then keeps those readings with the original progress photos instead of reducing the experience to a result screen you never see again.

The comparison view is the real differentiator. Select any two dates and GainFrame aligns the photos, calculates the body fat and score changes, maps muscle-group movement, and gives Coach the context needed to explain the result. Hevy, Strava, Apple Health, sleep, and recovery data can become part of that same progress story.

The accuracy evidence is now broader than the original one-person DEXA comparison. In GainFrame's [36-photo DEXA benchmark](/blog/ai-body-fat-estimator-accuracy-dexa-test/), the production configuration averaged a 3.77 percentage-point miss and landed within five points on 29 of 36 clean photos. The honest caveat is that this was an internal test using a female-only dataset, not an independent clinical trial.

**Best for:** lifters running a cut, bulk, or recomp who want to judge direction across months. **Limitations:** iOS only; estimates are not medical measurements; the feature depth is unnecessary if you want one number once.

**Our verdict:** best overall. [Download GainFrame on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gainframe-gym-progress-photos/id6759252082), or [try one browser scan first](/tools/body-fat-from-photo/).

## 2. Is Spren the best research-backed body composition scanner?

**Platform:** [iPhone](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spren-body-composition-scanner/id1664503999) | **Price:** Free download with subscription | **US rating:** 4.74 from 1,486 ratings

<figure className="post-inline-screenshot scroll-reveal"><img src="assets/spren.webp" alt="Spren body composition scan showing body fat, lean mass, android fat, and gynoid fat estimates" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Spren is built around a guided composition scan and a report-style result.</figcaption></figure>

[Spren](https://www.spren.com/) is the strongest choice for someone who values a composition-first report and a public research trail. Its guided scan estimates body fat, fat mass, lean mass, android and gynoid fat, resting metabolic rate, and healthy-range context. The experience feels closer to a portable assessment than a progress photo diary.

That focus creates the tradeoff. Spren tells you more about composition than most simple photo scanners, but it is less useful for judging individual muscle groups or keeping a visual transformation archive. Its own App Store description also states that images are processed in the cloud, which may matter if on-device photo storage is a priority.

**Best for:** general health users who want a research-oriented body composition report. **Limitations:** subscription for full use, lighter visual timeline, and results still depend on scan setup outside controlled research conditions.

## How accurate are AI body fat apps compared with DEXA?

The right answer is **useful for ranges and trends, imperfect for exact numbers**. A peer-reviewed 1,273-person study found strong agreement between one photo-based AI method and DEXA, but that result does not automatically transfer to every app. Models, training data, photo requirements, and real-world lighting all differ.

GainFrame's current internal benchmark gives a more product-specific answer: a 3.77-point average miss across 36 clean DEXA-paired photos, with roughly four out of five estimates within five points. That is useful enough to separate a meaningful cut from a flat trend. It is not close enough to claim that every 18% result is really 18%.

Use the same app, pose, lighting, clothing, framing, and time of day. Judge four to eight weeks of movement rather than one decimal point. If you need a clinical baseline, bone density, or regional tissue measurements, get a DEXA scan.
