## What is MacroFactor?

MacroFactor is a calorie and macro tracking app from the team behind Stronger By Science, built around one central idea: your calorie target should come from your own data, not from a formula's guess about people shaped like you.

Every mainstream tracker starts the same way — it estimates your maintenance calories from age, height, weight, and an activity multiplier. Those formulas commonly miss by hundreds of calories for individuals, which is why so many people eat their "deficit" for six weeks and lose nothing. Most apps stop there. MacroFactor doesn't: it watches your logged intake against your actual weight trend, recalculates your real energy expenditure, and updates your targets weekly.

At the time of writing, its App Store listing shows a rating around 4.8 across roughly 19,500 ratings — strong for a subscription-only app in a category where people mostly download the free option. The app is free to download with a trial; ongoing use requires a subscription, commonly around $12 per month with cheaper annual pricing (check the listing for current terms).

## What does MacroFactor do well?

-   **The adaptive TDEE algorithm is the headline, and it earns it.** The app treats your weight trend as the ground truth and adjusts targets to keep you on your chosen rate of loss or gain. Users commonly praise exactly this: the app does the recalculating that a coach would, weekly, without being asked. If your metabolism runs high or low of the formulas, MacroFactor finds out and corrects.
-   **Logging is genuinely fast.** Barcode scanning, a large verified food database, AI-assisted entry, and a layout tuned to minimize taps. Speed matters more than any other logging feature, because friction is why most people quit tracking — commonly within the first couple of weeks.
-   **The no-judgment design philosophy.** MacroFactor deliberately avoids red numbers, streak-shaming, and "you exceeded your budget" scolding. Miss a day and the algorithm absorbs it rather than lecturing you. For anyone with a complicated history with food tracking, this is commonly cited by users as the reason they stuck with this app after abandoning others.
-   **It handles the messy middle of a diet.** Metabolic adaptation — your expenditure drifting down as you diet — silently breaks static-target trackers. Because MacroFactor re-estimates expenditure continuously, the targets keep pace with the body they're aimed at.

## Where does MacroFactor fall short?

-   **Subscription-only, no free tier.** This is the most commonly noted complaint in reviews, and it's fair. MyFitnessPal-class competitors offer a workable free tier; MacroFactor's trial ends and then you pay. Around $12/month is real money for an app you must use daily to get value from.
-   **Logging discipline is still on you.** The algorithm is only as good as its inputs. Skip logging for a week and the expenditure estimate degrades with the data. If you already know you won't weigh food or log meals consistently, our [cutting without counting calories guide](/blog/cutting-without-counting-calories/) covers the honest alternatives — and they beat paying for a tracker you'll abandon.
-   **It's a numbers instrument, not a coach.** MacroFactor tells you what to eat in grams and calories; it doesn't tell you whether the plan is working aesthetically, and it has no opinion about your training.
-   **Overkill for casual goals.** If you just want to lose a few pounds without precision, a simpler approach — or no tracker at all — may serve you better than a tool built for people who want the math done properly.

## Is MacroFactor better than MyFitnessPal?

This is the comparison most people are actually weighing, so here's the honest version:

| | MyFitnessPal | MacroFactor |
|---|---|---|
| Free tier | Yes, workable | No — trial, then subscription |
| Calorie target | Static formula, manual adjustment | Recalculated weekly from your weight trend |
| Food database | Enormous, mixed verification | Large, verification commonly praised |
| Tone | Budget/streak framing | Deliberately judgment-free |
| Best for | Casual tracking, social features | Coached cuts, bulks, and recomps |

The structural difference is who does the adjusting. MyFitnessPal hands you a number and leaves the plan maintenance to you; MacroFactor treats target-setting as its job. For anyone running a deliberate cut or lean bulk — where being 300 calories off for two months actually matters — the adaptive approach is the better tool. Understanding your real [maintenance calories](/blog/maintenance-calories/) is the foundation either way, and MacroFactor is essentially that process automated.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is MacroFactor worth it?

If you're going to log food consistently, yes — the adaptive algorithm that recalculates targets from your weight trend is a genuine upgrade over static-formula trackers, and it's why we recommend it across our nutrition guides. If you won't log most meals most days, no tracker is worth paying for; the algorithm is only as good as the data you feed it.

### How much does MacroFactor cost?

MacroFactor is subscription-only — commonly around $12 per month, with annual plans that work out meaningfully cheaper, and a free trial to start. There's no permanent free tier, which is the most common complaint in reviews. Pricing changes, so check the current App Store listing before subscribing.

### Is MacroFactor better than MyFitnessPal?

For coached weight change, generally yes. MyFitnessPal sets a static calorie target from a formula and leaves adjustments to you; MacroFactor recalculates your targets weekly from your logged intake and weight trend. MyFitnessPal's advantages are its free tier and enormous social ecosystem. If you want the app to do the adjusting, MacroFactor is the stronger tool.

### How does MacroFactor's adaptive TDEE algorithm work?

You log food and body weight; the app compares calories in against your actual weight trend to estimate your true daily energy expenditure, then updates your calorie and macro targets weekly. Because the estimate comes from your data rather than a population formula, it absorbs metabolic differences and adapts as your body changes during a cut or bulk.

### Does MacroFactor work for bulking as well as cutting?

Yes — the algorithm is direction-agnostic. You set a goal rate of gain or loss and it adjusts targets to keep your actual weight trend on that rate. For lean bulking that's genuinely useful, since the line between productive surplus and fat gain is narrow. Pair it with a visual tracking layer to confirm the gained weight looks like muscle.
