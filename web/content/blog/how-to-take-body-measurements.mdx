A shoulder measurement can be 18 inches across or over 40 inches around. Both readings can come from the same person. Write “width” or “circumference” beside the number before you try to compare it. For this ten-site log, every row records a circumference.

## How do you set up a repeatable measurement?

Use a flexible, non-stretch tape marked in a unit you can read easily. A mirror helps you spot a tape that dips behind your back. Keep your posture and breathing comfortable and leave enough room for the tape to lie flat against your skin.

Take each reading, remove the tape and place it again. If the readings differ, check the landmark, tape tension, side and position. Record a representative repeat reading and a short note about your method. Try the same time of day and a similar point relative to meals and training when you measure again.

For the limbs, run the tape around the limb at a right angle to its length. For the hips, stand with your feet together and check from the side that the tape reaches the fullest point of the buttocks. Choose one reproducible chest level and record the reading after a normal exhalation.

## How do you measure shoulder girth versus shoulder width?

For girth, stand with arms relaxed and place the tape around the widest part of both deltoids, across your chest and upper back. Ask someone to check the tape behind you. Use this value only where a guide or formula calls for circumference.

[Shoulder width](/blog/average-shoulder-width/) is a straight-line distance. Measurements between the bony shoulder points and measurements across the outer deltoids also describe different widths. A tape wrapped around the body cannot give you either distance. The distinction matters if you use a [shoulder-to-waist ratio](/blog/shoulder-to-waist-ratio/).

## Which waist landmark belongs in your log?

For a comparison with US NHANES data, use the site just above the upper edge of your right hip bone at the side of your torso. Keep the tape horizontal and read it after a normal exhalation. See the official [CDC procedure, section 3.4.8](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhanes/public/2021/manuals/2021-Anthropometry-Procedures-Manual-508.pdf#page=47) or the landmark photos and population tables for [women](/blog/average-waist-size-women/) and [men](/blog/average-waist-size-men/).

If you have an existing log measured through your navel, keep that series labeled “navel.” Start a separate row when changing sites. When a [waist-to-height ratio](/blog/waist-to-height-ratio/), [waist-to-hip ratio](/blog/waist-to-hip-ratio/) or [tape-based body-fat formula](/blog/how-to-measure-body-fat-with-tape/) specifies a site, follow its instructions before entering a value.

## How do you record four comparable dates?

[Download the one-page, four-date measurement log (PDF)](assets/four-date-measurement-log.pdf). Print it or mark it up on your device. Fill in the unit, side, upper-arm pose and waist landmark once at the top. Enter the actual date in each column before writing the circumference next to its site.

If the setup changes, make a note at the bottom. For example, a set of arm measurements taken after training may not line up with previous readings taken before training. A monthly full session gives you a repeatable starting schedule. You can log one site more often if that suits your goal.

The [body measurement photo tool](/tools/body-measurements-calculator/) offers a separate view of body proportions from a front-facing picture. It takes a photo as input. Keep the tape measurements in your log. An image cannot verify the exact circumference or explain what contributed to a change.

## What can you learn from the change between two dates?

A change in inches records a difference in size at the chosen site. Tape tension, positioning and recent exercise can affect the reading. The number alone cannot distinguish muscle, fat or fluid. When other conditions match, several similar readings give you a better comparison than a single large swing.

If your [measurements change while your weight stays the same](/blog/why-am-i-losing-inches-but-not-weight/), compare your notes about the setup. If you keep progress photos, date them and compare the same angles. The [tape versus smart scale guide](/blog/tape-measure-vs-smart-scale/) covers a second way of recording change. For a digital log, compare the [body scanning and measurement apps](/blog/best-body-scanning-measurement-apps/).

## What else should you know about using a tape measure?

### Should you flex when taking body measurements?

For a general tracking log, stand relaxed. If you want to track a flexed upper arm, record the arm, elbow position and whether you flexed it. A relaxed measurement and a flexed measurement can each have their own row or record. Repeat the same version next time.

### Where should I put the tape to measure my waist?

For the NHANES survey comparison, place a horizontal tape just above the top of the right hip bone at your side. For a personal navel-level log, label that site explicitly. A ratio, calculator or clinical protocol can specify another location, so check its instructions before using your number.

### How do you measure shoulder width and shoulder circumference?

Shoulder circumference is the distance around both deltoids and across the upper chest and back. A shoulder-width measurement is a straight distance between two specified points. The units can look identical in a log. Record the name of the method as well as the number before making comparisons.

### How often should I take body measurements?

Choose dates you can repeat under similar conditions. A monthly full-body session is a practical starting schedule. If a particular measurement matters for a training goal, you can record it more often and keep the other rows blank. A change is easier to interpret when the setup stays consistent.

### Can you take body measurements by yourself?

You can usually position a soft tape around your limbs or waist by yourself. Use a mirror to check that the tape lies flat all the way around. A second person is useful for chest and shoulder circumference because it is easy to move the tape as you reach behind you.
