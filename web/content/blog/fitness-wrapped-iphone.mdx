On iPhone, allow the workout categories you want to include, open the yearly wrap and check the dates before sharing. To add a visual diary, choose a few check-in photos and export a separate GIF.</p></div>

A year of workouts fits on a few recap cards. A year of photos is harder to sort. Start with the workout story. It gives you the dates to look for when you open your camera roll.

## Where do you get Fitness Wrapped on iPhone?

Look for [Fitness Wrapped by Onlabs in the US App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitness-wrapped/id6739229787). Install it on the iPhone that has your workout history. When it asks for Health access, review the categories and allow the ones you want in your recap. You can check which apps can read your data from your profile in the Health app.

Open the yearly wrap and read the year on the card. The developer lists December 1 as the opening date for the 2026 story. A recap you share in December can cover a year that still has workouts left in it. Keep the date of your export if you plan to compare it with a later version.

## Why might a workout be missing?

Find one specific session you remember, such as a Sunday walk. Look for it in your workout history before diagnosing the whole year. If only your training app shows the session, check whether that app saved it to Health. If Health shows it, check Fitness Wrapped’s read permission and the period you selected.

An Apple Watch is optional. Another app or compatible device can write workouts to Apple Health. An iPhone step total and a saved walking workout are different records. A day with several thousand steps can reasonably contribute no walking workout to a recap.

## What can you save for free?

The developer lists current-month and current-year wraps, workout share cards and one active matchup in the free tier. Previous wraps and full history are Premium features. Check the offer on your device if you want to open an older year. You can save a single page as an image when one activity tells the story, or save the wrap as a video. ## How do you make a photo recap to go with your stats?

1. Pick three to twelve check-ins from your own library. Try one early, one middle and one recent image before sorting through every month.
2. Open the [progress photo timelapse maker](/tools/progress-photo-timelapse-maker/) and add the photos. Choose JPEG, PNG or WebP. If a file is HEIC, export a JPEG copy first.
3. Put the photos in date order. Align a fixed point, such as your shoulders, and leave a similar margin above your head. Use the privacy covers if you want them baked into the output.
4. Select **Create GIF** for a loop or **Save contact sheet** for a single image. Open the prepared file and save your copy before you close the tab. You will have a workout recap and a separate photo file to share together or keep private.

## Which details should you check before sharing?

Read each workout card for routes, dates and locations you would prefer to keep private. For photos, inspect the mirror, the room behind you and anyone else in the frame. Open the saved GIF once and watch a full loop. A detail covered in the first frame can still appear in the third.

## What else should you know?

### Is Fitness Wrapped an Apple feature?

<p>Fitness Wrapped is a separate iPhone app from Onlabs. It asks for permission to read the workout data that Apple Health holds. You can use Apple’s Fitness app for its own workout history or open Fitness Wrapped when you want the developer’s shareable story format.</p>

### Will gym sessions from another app appear?

<p>Look up a particular gym session in Apple Health first. If it was saved as a workout there, Fitness Wrapped can use the data you authorize it to read. A set of notes or a plan that lives only inside a lifting app has a different source.</p>

### Can I make a recap with just three photos?

<p>Three photos are enough for the browser timelapse maker. Pick one from each part of the year that you want to remember. You can reorder them, place them in the same portrait frame and export a contact sheet first to check the order before creating the animation.</p>

### Does my photo recap need to go online?

<p>The photo tool prepares the GIF and contact sheet in your browser tab. Save the output to your own library and close the page when you are finished. Sending the file to someone or posting it to a network is a separate step that you control.</p>

### What if I only want one shareable picture?

<p>Choose a single card from the workout story, or export the photo contact sheet for a still image containing several check-ins. Open the result at phone size before you send it. If the dates are difficult to read, use fewer frames or share the workout card separately.</p>
