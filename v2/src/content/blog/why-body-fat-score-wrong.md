<p class="post-lede">You take a progress photo after a solid 8-week cut. You feel leaner. Your clothes
fit better. You step on the scale and you're down 6 pounds. Then the AI scores your photo and gives
you a 52 — lower than last month.</p>

<p>Your first thought: <em>"I'm not making progress."</em> But that's probably wrong. The score dropped
because of the photo, not your body.</p>

<p>Most people don't realize how dramatically lighting, camera angle, and distance affect any visual
body composition reading. A harsh overhead light can erase visible muscle definition. A slightly
off-center angle can flatten your V-taper into nothing. Stand two feet farther from the mirror and
your proportions compress.</p>

<p>The problem isn't the AI. The problem is your photo setup changed between sessions, and the AI is
faithfully scoring what it actually sees.</p>

<h2>The two things that affect your body fat score</h2>

<p>Every visual body composition score is influenced by exactly two categories of factors. Understanding
them changes how you interpret every reading you get.</p>

<h3>Photo factors (things that aren't your body)</h3>

<p>These have nothing to do with your physique, but they can swing your score by 10–15 points:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Lighting:</strong> Flat overhead light erases shadows and hides muscle separation. Side
lighting or natural light from a window dramatically improves visible definition.</li>
<li><strong>Camera angle:</strong> A straight-on front shot shows your shoulder width honestly.
Tilting the camera even 10 degrees can flatten your frame or exaggerate your midsection.</li>
<li><strong>Distance:</strong> Stand too far from the camera and fine details like vascularity,
striations, and ab separation disappear. Stand too close and proportions distort.</li>
<li><strong>Clothing:</strong> Loose tanks and baggy shorts hide your actual structure. Fitted
clothing or minimal clothing reveals what the AI needs to see.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Physique factors (things that are your body)</h3>

<p>These are the real signals that reflect your training and nutrition:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Visible body fat:</strong> Subcutaneous fat around the midsection, love handles, and
face.</li>
<li><strong>Muscle development:</strong> Fullness, separation, and definition across shoulders,
chest, arms, back, core, and legs.</li>
<li><strong>Proportions:</strong> How your muscle groups relate to each other — V-taper,
shoulder-to-waist ratio, upper vs. lower body balance.</li>
<li><strong>Vascularity and striations:</strong> Fine-detail indicators of low body fat and muscle
maturity.</li>
</ul>

<p>If your photo setup is inconsistent, the AI will confuse photo factors with physique factors. Your
score fluctuates, and you lose confidence in the trend.</p>

<h2>The coaching tip that tells you exactly what to do next</h2>

<p>Every Score Insights breakdown ends with a personalized <strong>Tip for Next Time</strong>. This
isn't generic advice like "eat less and exercise more." It's specific to what the AI saw in your
photo.</p>

<p>For example: <em>"Try a side-profile shot next time to show off your chest thickness and arm
development."</em></p>

<p>The tip might target your photo technique (better angle, better lighting) or your training (focus on
a lagging muscle group to push your score higher next session). Either way, you walk away knowing
exactly what to change.</p>

<hr class="post-divider">

<h2>Quick checklist: standardize your progress photos</h2>

<p>The simplest way to get accurate, comparable scores every time:</p>

<ul>
<li>Same location, same time of day</li>
<li>Natural side lighting from a window — avoid direct overhead lights</li>
<li>Camera at chest height, 4–5 feet away</li>
<li>Same minimal clothing every session</li>
<li>Fasted or at the same time relative to meals</li>
<li>Same poses in the same order</li>
</ul>

<p>Consistency in your photo setup turns your score trend into something you can actually trust over
weeks and months.</p>

<hr class="post-divider">

<h2>Stop guessing. Start reading the breakdown.</h2>

<p>A single number without context is noise. A score with a breakdown telling you exactly what
influenced it is data.</p>

<p>Here's the framework:</p>

<ol class="post-steps">
<li><strong>Take your photo</strong> using the standardized checklist above.</li>
<li><strong>Check your score</strong> — but don't react to the number alone.</li>
<li><strong>Read the breakdown.</strong> Were Photo Factors flagged? That's a setup issue, not a
body issue.</li>
<li><strong>Focus on Physique Observations.</strong> That's your actual progress.</li>
<li><strong>Follow the coaching tip.</strong> Improve your photo technique or target a lagging
muscle group.</li>
<li><strong>Reassess every 4–8 weeks</strong> using consistent photos. The trend is what matters.
</li>
</ol>
