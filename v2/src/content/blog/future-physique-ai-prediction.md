<p>Beyond the visuals and numbers, every prediction includes two things that make it actionable:
<strong>key improvement areas</strong> (which muscle groups will change the most) and
<strong>recommended focus areas</strong> (specific training adjustments to maximize results).
</p>

<hr class="post-divider">

<h2>How it works</h2>

<p>The prediction engine uses your actual progress photo history — not a one-time snapshot. Here's the
process:</p>

<ol class="post-steps">
<li>
<strong>Select a pose.</strong> You pick a pose template — Front, Front Flexed, Side, etc. The
AI needs at least three photos of the same pose taken across different dates to establish a
trajectory.
</li>
<li>
<strong>Choose a time horizon.</strong> You can project 3 months, 6 months, or 1 year ahead.
Each horizon generates its own prediction and future image.
</li>
<li>
<strong>AI analyzes your rate of change.</strong> The engine examines your progress photos in
chronological order — looking at how your muscle development, body fat, definition, and
proportions have changed over time. It identifies your rate of improvement for each body region.
</li>
<li>
<strong>Future image generation.</strong> Based on your trajectory, the AI generates a modified
version of your most recent photo showing the predicted visual changes — more definition,
different proportions, reduced body fat — at the selected time horizon.
</li>
<li>
<strong>Stats + recommendations.</strong> The report includes projected body composition numbers
and specific, actionable training recommendations for your weakest areas.
</li>
</ol>

<p>The whole process takes about 15 seconds. Every prediction is saved, so you can come back and see how
your actual progress compared to the AI's projection.</p>

<h2>How accurate are the AI physique predictions?</h2>

<p>Let's be honest about what this is and isn't.</p>

<p><strong>What it is:</strong> a projection based on your demonstrated rate of change. If you've been
consistently improving your shoulder development over 6 months of photos, the AI extrapolates that
trend forward. The more data points you have (more photos over a longer period), the more reliable
the projection.</p>

<p><strong>What it isn't:</strong> a guarantee. The prediction assumes you maintain your current
training and nutrition. If you take a month off, switch programs, or change your diet significantly,
your actual results will diverge from the prediction.</p>

<p>That said, the value isn't in pixel-perfect accuracy. It's in <strong>visualization and
motivation</strong>. Seeing a plausible version of your future self — backed by your actual data
— is a genuinely powerful motivator. It answers the question "Is this worth it?" with a visual yes.
</p>

<blockquote class="post-callout scroll-reveal">
<p>The best part: you can revisit your predictions later and compare them against reality. Did the
AI overestimate your chest development but nail your shoulder trajectory? That tells you
something about where your training is working and where it needs adjustment.</p>
</blockquote>

<h2>What you need to get started</h2>

<p>Future Physique requires at least <strong>three progress photos of the same pose</strong>, spread
across different dates. The AI needs enough data to establish a trajectory — one photo is a
snapshot, two is a comparison, but three or more is a trend.</p>

<p>If you don't have three photos yet, that's fine. Import your camera roll (GainFrame's batch import
handles this automatically), or start taking weekly progress photos. Within a few weeks, you'll have
enough data for your first prediction.</p>

<ol class="post-tips">
<li><strong>More photos = better predictions.</strong> The AI improves as it gets more data points.
10+ photos over 3+ months gives the strongest projections.</li>
<li><strong>Same pose matters.</strong> The AI compares matching poses — don't mix front and side
photos. Use GainFrame's template system to keep them organized.</li>
<li><strong>Try different timeframes.</strong> Start with 6 months for the most balanced prediction.
Use 3 months for near-term motivation, 1 year for big-picture planning.</li>
</ol>
