<h2>Method 4: Strength progressions</h2>

<p>If your big lifts are going up, you're almost certainly gaining muscle. Progressive overload is
the driver of hypertrophy — so tracking your lifts is an indirect but reliable muscle gain
indicator.</p>

<p>The limitation: strength gains can come from neural adaptations (especially in beginners), better
form, or improved recovery — not just muscle growth. Strength alone doesn't tell you
<em>where</em> you're gaining or whether you're also losing fat.
</p>

<p>That's why the best approach combines strength tracking with visual evidence. Apps like
<a href="/blog/hevy-app-gainframe-integration">Hevy</a> track your workout volume, and
GainFrame auto-attaches that data to the progress photo taken on the same day — so you can
correlate volume increases with visible changes.
</p>

<hr class="post-divider">

<h2>Method 5: Clothing fit and visual cues</h2>

<p>Don't underestimate the basics. If your shirts are tighter in the shoulders but looser in the
waist, you're gaining muscle and losing fat. If your watch band needs loosening because your
forearms grew — that's real progress.</p>

<p>The problem with this method: it's imprecise and hard to track over time. But combined with
photos and AI data, it's a useful gut check.</p>

<hr class="post-divider">

<h2>The best approach: combine methods</h2>

<p>No single method tells the full story. Here's what we recommend:</p>

<ol class="post-tips">
<li><strong>Take weekly progress photos.</strong> Same pose, same lighting. This is your primary
data source for tracking muscle gain.</li>
<li><strong>Run AI body comp monthly.</strong> Tap your photo and get body fat %, FFMI, and
muscle group scores. Track the trend, not individual readings.</li>
<li><strong>Weigh yourself daily, average weekly.</strong> The scale isn't useless — it just
needs context. A weekly average removes water noise.</li>
<li><strong>Track your lifts.</strong> Progressive overload confirms you're stimulating growth.
Pair with photo data for the complete picture.</li>
<li><strong>Check your predictions quarterly.</strong> Run an AI physique prediction every 3
months. Compare your actual progress to the projection and adjust your program.</li>
</ol>

<p>The scale measures gravity's pull on your body. Your phone camera, combined with AI, measures
<strong>what your body is actually made of</strong> — and where it's headed.
</p>
