### Headline observations

1. **Review-count gap is enormous.** Progress (Lasmit) has **15,000 reviews vs GainFrame's 20** — a 750× asymmetry. trackBod has 46×. Spren has 75×. **Trust-signal recovery is a multi-year problem, not a campaign.**

2. **GainFrame's pricing is competitive but not the cheapest.** $39.99/yr beats Spren ($69.99-$119.99/yr) and FitnessAI ($9.99/mo = $119.88/yr if no annual discount), but **trackBod ($29.99/yr) and Metamorph ($29.99/yr) are both 25% cheaper**. Snapsie is $0. **Pricing is not a moat — feature differentiation is.**

3. **Three of six are App Store-only** (trackBod, Metamorph, Snapsie). **Three have substantial websites** (Spren, FitnessAI, Progress). The website-based ones have massive content footprints — Spren has 54 blog posts + B2B sales motion, FitnessAI has 80+ blogs + 500 exercise pages, Progress has 40+ blogs covering definitional + weight-loss content.

4. **Two competitors cite the SAME validation paper** — Spren and BodyScan/FitnessAI both cite Nature DOI s41430-024-01424-w. Worth investigating whether they share underlying technology or both reference it as a benchmark. Either way: **two competitors have published peer-reviewed accuracy validation; GainFrame has not.**

5. **Snapsie is effectively abandoned** — last updated August 2017 (9 years ago). Still surfaces in SERPs due to App Store domain authority + FitMenCook backing. **Treat as a SERP placeholder, not an active competitor.**

6. **Metamorph's anti-AI positioning is the most defined attack on GainFrame.** Their "we don't even have access to your photos" framing is a deliberate dig at cloud-AI apps. GainFrame's Gemini dependency is honest but real — this is a comparison-article battlefield.
