# Founder post outline — copycats showing up while building in public

Status: OUTLINE ONLY (not drafted, not published)
Target: web/content/blog/ founder-story lane (same shelf as spent-5k-on-app-ads, organic-traffic-15x-90-days)
Voice: /mike-writes — flat declarative title, lead with the fact, numbers carry it

## Title options (flat, no teaser hooks)

1. "A competitor republished my content calendar, title for title, nine days after I did"
2. "My app has copycats now"
3. "Building in public got my blog cloned. I'm still doing it."

## Facts available to use (all verified, cite carefully)

- My comparison/roundup posts committed May 1–12, 2026 (Spren review, trackBod review,
  fitness app stack, 5 AI physique rating apps, FFMI chart, jawline body fat, GLP-1 apps).
- May 21, 2026: a competitor site published 40+ posts in a single day, ~20 of them
  near-identical titles to mine ("Spren App Review 2026", "trackBod Review 2026",
  "The Best Fitness App Stack for 2026", "Body Composition for Lifters", etc.),
  plus a "them vs GainFrame" comparison page.
- Their domain: registered June 22, 2025 — the product idea predates mine (honesty beat:
  I can't claim the idea was stolen; GainFrame's first commit is Feb 2026). What got
  cloned is the marketing: the exact content map, the listicle-with-yourself-in-it
  format, the vs-pages.
- Their bodies are thin AI rewrites, not verbatim copies of my prose. Titles and topics
  aren't copyrightable. There is no lawsuit here and the post shouldn't pretend there is.
- Their brand terms: 15 impressions, 0 clicks on my GSC since April. Mine: (pull current
  clicks/day number at draft time for contrast).
- No iOS app from them yet ("coming soon" on their site). Mine ships weekly.
- I already published a "[their name] alternatives" post (Jul 9) — the counter-move.
- Softer signals I can only describe, not prove: same accounts appearing under my
  build-in-public posts asking "how did you do X", new accounts with no history asking
  pointed pipeline questions. Frame as "I can't prove who's asking, and that's the point."

## Spine (founder-post framing: outcome first, confession as texture)

1. **Lead with the fact.** I share everything about how I grow my app — the ad spend,
   the SEO playbook, the traffic numbers. Last month a competitor republished my blog's
   entire content map, title for title, nine days after my posts went live.
2. **The discovery moment.** How I found it (their vs-my-app page showing up). The
   gut-drop. "Nerve wracking" is the honest word — keep it.
3. **What copying actually looked like.** The receipts, stated plainly: the title list
   side by side, the one-day 40-post dump, the batch timestamps. Let the reader draw
   the conclusion; don't editorialize with "shameless" etc.
4. **The honest undercut (trust engine).** Their domain is older than my app. The idea
   isn't mine alone and never was. AI body analysis has a dozen players. What stung
   wasn't competition — it was watching my specific words come back with the serial
   numbers filed off.
5. **Why I'm not stopping build-in-public.** The math: sharing got me X clicks/day,
   the community, the comparisons that rank. The clone got them 15 impressions.
   Copying the artifact doesn't copy the engine — they can take a title but they
   can't take the DEXA study, the 189 survey responses, the weekly release cadence,
   or the reason readers trust the posts (I use the products I review).
6. **What I'm changing anyway (mechanism, not vague "being careful").**
   - Publish the numbers, delay the playbook: results immediately, mechanics 4–6 weeks
     later once the compounding is banked.
   - Answer questions in public threads, not DMs — if it's worth sharing it's worth
     sharing with everyone, and it stops alt-account farming quietly.
   - Wrote the alternatives post so anyone searching their brand finds my honest
     comparison. Fair play cuts both ways.
7. **Close (short declaratives, self-deprecating, no slogan).** Something in the shape
   of: "Maybe they out-execute me. That part was always true, copycats or not. The
   posts are still mine, the app still ships every week, and the readers can tell
   which one of us took the photos. Happy to answer questions — in the comments."

## Decisions to make before drafting

- **Name them or not?** Recommendation: don't name or link. Describe the pattern;
  anyone motivated can find them, and a link/namecheck is free distribution + a
  possible defamation footgun. The alternatives post already handles the SEO angle.
- **Screenshot the title-for-title diff?** Strong visual, but it identifies them.
  If we don't name, use a redacted/blurred side-by-side.
- **Where it ships:** blog + Reddit r/SideProject or r/EntrepreneurRideAlong cross-post
  (this is founder-audience content, not GainFrame-user content).
- Pull fresh traffic numbers at draft time; don't reuse stale ones from this outline.

## What NOT to do in this post

- No accusations of illegality — nothing here is actionable and claiming theft of an
  "idea" that predates the app would get picked apart in comments.
- No "how to protect yourself from copycats" listicle turn — keep it a story.
- No AI-slop tells: no "here's what I learned 👇", no manufactured aphorism closer.
