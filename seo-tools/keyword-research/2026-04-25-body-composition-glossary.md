## TL;DR — A surprising inversion

The gap analysis from `competitor-scan` suggested "what is BMI / lean body mass / muscle mass percentage" as the highest-priority content cluster (Progress by Lasmit owns this lane). **SERP analysis reveals the opposite is true for the popular-definitional terms.**

The most-searched definitional queries are **HEAVILY LOCKED by hospital/health authorities** (CDC, NIH, Cleveland Clinic, Mayo Clinic, WebMD, Healthline, Harvard) — these scored 85-95 difficulty with ZERO winnable SERP positions for a small site:

- `what is bmi` — 95 difficulty (CDC ×2, NIH, Cleveland Clinic, Kaiser, American Cancer Society, etc.)
- `what is body composition` — 85 difficulty (UC Davis, WebMD, ScienceDirect, MIT)
- `what is waist to hip ratio` — 95 difficulty (WebMD, Healthline, Wikipedia, Harvard)
- `what is body composition scan` — 95 difficulty (UC Davis ×2, PMC ×2, UVA Radiology)

**But gym-native specialized terms have CLEAN SERPs** because health authorities don't focus on them — only fitness-niche sites and a few research papers compete:

- `what is ffmi` — **55 difficulty** (gym calculators + 1 PMC paper, no Cleveland Clinic / WebMD / Healthline)
- `ffmi chart` — **50 difficulty**
- `lean mass vs muscle mass` — **60 difficulty**
- `what is body fat percentage vs bmi` — **75 difficulty** (only Healthline as tier-1; comparison intent)

**Strategic insight:** GainFrame's gym-native brand voice (FFMI, recomp, lean mass) is actually a SEO advantage — it lets us target specialized terms the health authority sites don't bother with. **The right move is NOT to chase the generic "what is BMI" cluster Progress (Lasmit) covers. The right move is to own the gym-native vocabulary cluster they don't.**
