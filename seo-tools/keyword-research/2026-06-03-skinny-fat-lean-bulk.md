## Strategic throughline

Both clusters converge on **the exact GainFrame thesis**: *the scale can't show you muscle-vs-fat — body composition can.*
- A "skinny fat" person has normal weight + normal BMI but high-ish body fat and low muscle. The scale says they're fine; their body comp says otherwise. GainFrame's photo → body-fat % + 12 muscle-group scores literally *is* the diagnostic.
- "Is my lean bulk working?" is the same question mid-surplus: am I adding muscle or just fat? The whole SERP pushes DEXA as the gold standard — which pairs directly with our existing "AI alternative to DEXA" content.

This is a coherent, on-brand content lane, not two disconnected topics.

- **Intent:** informational (diagnostic)
- **Recommended skill:** `blog-post-generator`
- **Suggested title:** "How to Tell If You're Skinny Fat (And Why the Scale Can't Tell You)"
- **Covers keywords:** how to tell if you're skinny fat · how to know if you're skinny fat · how to check if you're skinny fat · how to know skinny fat · am I skinny fat
- **Why opportunity:** Highest-fit keyword in either cluster. A normal-BMI / high-fat / low-muscle person is invisible to the scale — GainFrame's photo → body-fat % + 12 muscle scores is the literal diagnostic. SERP has no authority lockout (the *definitional* heads do — see Skipped). Honest framing: skinny-fat is a body-composition problem, AI photo estimate is ~2-4% of DEXA, not a medical diagnosis.
- **Existing coverage:** None. Cross-link to `/blog/body-fat-percentage-vs-bmi/`, `/blog/visceral-fat-vs-subcutaneous-fat/`.
