### N1. Long femurs and squat proportions

The existing torso guide measures sitting height. A reader asking about thigh leverage and a side-on squat still needs a different demonstration. That article had two disclosed impressions across two femur questions, one on the exact phrase. The new guide would show repeatable external landmarks, distinguish an approximate segment ratio from actual bone length, and draw two different segment arrangements at the same depth. A squat photo alone cannot determine someone's anatomy. The business job is discovery in the body proportions cluster, followed by a visit to the torso guide or the measurement guide.

US mobile Google returned an AI Overview, images, related questions and coaching/video results. The Body Mentors guide was organic #2, a form-check discussion #3 and a leverage visualizer #5. Readers already have generic “body type” advice. A clear side-view diagram with scale and camera-position limitations earns its place. Link from `long-torso-short-legs` (“compare thigh and torso length for a squat”) and `how-to-take-body-measurements` (“where thigh-length measurements start and end”). [Coaching result](https://www.bodymentors.co.uk/blog/what-your-squat-says-about-your-femurs/) · [Original research in resistance-trained adults](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10376130/).

### N2. One arm looks bigger

The average-bicep reference drew 2,610 Google clicks in 90 days; the new reader is comparing two arms on the same person. The site has one impression for “one bicep bigger than the other” on the general bicep article and another for a different-size question on the skinny-arms article. The general lagging-muscle guide covers a whole-body review. Show relaxed and flexed conditions, left/right tape readings at the same landmark and a separately labeled performance check. A photo cannot establish weakness or diagnose swelling. The intended action is a personal comparison on `/tools/progress-photo-compare/`.

US Google mobile showed AI Overview, images, questions, video and several forum threads. Chris Heria's 2024 article was the first organic result. Pose, camera angle, handedness and size versus strength recur. An original same-person camera example and reproducible sheet make the question testable at home. Link from `average-bicep-size` (“check a left-to-right arm difference”) and `how-to-identify-lagging-muscle-groups` (“compare both arms under the same conditions”). [Leading practical guide](https://chrisheria.com/blogs/news/2) · [Original strength-asymmetry research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9319678/).

### N3. A blind progress-photo reveal

Three pairs, dates hidden. Readers guess the order, reveal the dates and see the relevant lighting, pose and camera information. Include one pair taken the same day. Only publish with verified dates and explicit permission for public use, or take a fresh purpose-made series. No invented before-and-after, no inference of muscle or fat gained. An accessible answer disclosure can sit in the article. Close with an invitation to line up two of the reader's own pictures in Progress Photo Compare.

Exact-phrase US volume was unavailable and public search did not establish a fitness-specific market. The existing picture chart sent 119 reader sessions to tools and 16 to the Store. The “what would I look like?” article sent 38 and 20 respectively. These are evidence for a visual format; they don't predict a quiz result. Judge N3 on comparison starts, successful comparisons and voluntary sharing. Link from `before-after-comparing-progress-photos` (“try reading a photo pair without the dates”) and `pump-vs-no-pump-progress-photos` (“see a same-day pair before the reveal”). Original editorial or social commission, distinct from the separately approved native iPhone how-to.

### N4. Grip strength you can retest

Pair the popular hand-size and forearm guides with a question about function. Current GSC disclosed two related impressions with adjacent articles and no traffic owner for the exact technique. Show handle adjustment, test posture, trial order, units and rest under one named protocol. Identify which readings can be compared. Keep any norms aligned to that protocol and population. The primary job is discovery and repeat use of a printable log. The existing hand-size guide produced just two Store-reaching sessions in 716 entries.

US Google mobile included an AI Overview, demonstrations, images, questions and forums. A clinic assessment page, StrongFirst discussion and encyclopedia entry led organic. Aim to beat the existing demonstrations on repeatability. Link from `average-hand-size` (“measure grip strength on a dynamometer”) and `average-forearm-size` (“record a repeatable grip-strength test”). Return to the hand-size reference. [Adult NHANES grip-test documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2011/DataFiles/MGX_G.htm) · [NHANES procedure](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhanes/public/2011/manuals/Muscle_Strength_Proc_Manual.pdf).

### N5. Time off lifting

A return-to-training timeline answers a distinct question from our lifting-start and recomp timelines. No disclosed current query rows in the tested detraining/regain family. A 90-day comparator, the recomp timeline, earned 208 Google clicks. Label the time sampled by each study, population and method. Put “what the study measured” next to “what you can track on your return.” A two-week holiday and prolonged immobilization are different scenarios. Avoid promising a week at which loss starts. Route returning readers to the muscle-gain tracking guide and the photo comparison tool.

US mobile search showed AI Overview, related questions and discussions. A popular clip, a workout forum and EVO Fitness led organic, so a bare number has little editorial value. The original figure would show a training, break and return sequence alongside separate strength and size measures. Link from `how-long-to-see-results-from-lifting` (“what changes after time off”) and `how-to-track-muscle-gain-progress` (“record your return after a training break”). [Controlled detraining and retraining study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/sms.14739).

### N6. Creatine and a sudden scale change

The water-weight and scale-fluctuation articles describe several sources of variability. The specific question here starts with a dated change: beginning creatine. One disclosed generic creatine impression, no identified owner for the exact search. Show how to record existing weight readings, waist, training and photos around that date. Clearly label worked examples, account for uncertainty and avoid attributing an individual's change to a single cause. A dose recommendation is outside this brief. Link from `water-weight` (“what changes when you start creatine”) and `why-does-my-weight-fluctuate` (“tracking water weight after starting creatine”). Return to the progress-photos-versus-scale guide.

US mobile Google showed AI Overview and related questions, with Naked Nutrition, Power and BUBS Naturals among the first organic entries. Retailers already answer the supplement question. GainFrame can show a log people can use to interpret their own trend. The adjacent weight-appearance article had 70 clicks in 28 days; no Store action was observed across its 100 entry sessions. This idea is primarily discovery. [Published body-water experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC155510/) · [Example of a ranking supplier article](https://nakednutrition.com/en-ie/blogs/supplements/does-creatine-make-you-gain-weight).

### N7. Making a next-session decision

The current resistance-training guide received scattered progressive-overload impressions, with no disclosed exact double-progression owner. Use four consecutive entries for one exercise with an explicitly hypothetical rep range, show how the decision changes with each set, and include a blank copy. Include an example where the next dumbbell is a large jump. The closest existing muscle-gain tracking page drew only one Google click in 90 days. This is an experiment in reaching lifters with a log, rather than a proven search franchise. Link from `resistance-training-guidelines` (“a worked double-progression example”) and `how-to-track-muscle-gain-progress` (“decide when to add reps or load”). Return to the tracking guide. Judge qualified reader activity before commissioning more training-programming pages.

An English web search found dated November guides, worked logs and printable protocols from several lifting apps. Exact mobile feature placement wasn't available for this supplemental check. The benefit must be immediate: a reader can copy the log and choose their next session. Distinguish a study of adding reps versus load from proof for any particular double-progression rule. [RIGOR example](https://joinrigor.com/guides/double-progression) · [Original repetition/load progression trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36199287/).
